# 🤝 Responsible AI Projects

A collection of hands-on projects exploring fairness, transparency, privacy, accountability, safety, and the responsible development and deployment of artificial intelligence systems.

This folder documents my journey toward building AI applications that are not only useful and technically sound, but also evaluated for their potential impact on people and society.

> **Current status:** Project roadmap. Projects will be marked as completed only after implementation, evaluation, and documentation.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Understand the core principles of Responsible AI.
- Identify and measure bias in datasets and model predictions.
- Evaluate model performance across relevant demographic groups.
- Explore fairness metrics and their limitations.
- Improve AI transparency and explainability.
- Protect personal and sensitive information.
- Evaluate LLM outputs for harmful, misleading, or discriminatory behavior.
- Assess privacy risks in AI applications.
- Design human oversight and accountability mechanisms.
- Document AI limitations, risks, and intended use.
- Build repeatable Responsible AI evaluation workflows.
- Understand the trade-offs between fairness, privacy, safety, and model utility.

## 📚 Core Concepts Covered

### 1. Fairness and Bias

- Dataset representation and sampling bias
- Historical and measurement bias
- Label quality and proxy variables
- Group-wise accuracy and error rates
- Demographic parity
- Equal opportunity and equalized odds
- Fairness trade-offs
- Intersectional analysis where sample sizes support it
- Limitations of fairness metrics

Fairness cannot be reduced to a single metric. Different definitions may conflict, and a model can perform similarly across groups while still producing harmful outcomes.

### 2. Transparency and Explainability

- Global versus local explanations
- Feature importance
- SHAP and LIME
- Model limitations
- Prediction confidence and uncertainty
- Model cards
- Dataset documentation
- Clear communication of AI-generated content

An explanation is not automatically proof that a model's decision is correct or fair. Explanations should be assessed for usefulness and stability.

### 3. Privacy and Data Protection

- Data minimization
- Personal and sensitive information
- Anonymization versus pseudonymization
- Re-identification risks
- Access control
- Data retention and deletion
- Synthetic data limitations
- Differential privacy fundamentals
- Privacy-utility trade-offs

Removing names or email addresses alone does not guarantee that a dataset is anonymous.

### 4. Safety and Robustness

- Invalid and unexpected inputs
- Out-of-distribution data
- Model uncertainty
- Safe fallback behavior
- Appropriate abstention
- Adversarial and misuse scenarios
- LLM hallucinations
- Prompt injection
- Tool permissions
- Human approval for consequential actions

### 5. Accountability and Human Oversight

- Intended use and prohibited use
- Stakeholder impact
- Human review and override
- Audit trails
- Incident reporting
- Risk ownership
- Corrective actions
- Monitoring after deployment
- Documentation of limitations

### 6. Responsible Generative AI

- Harmful content evaluation
- Stereotype and bias analysis
- Multilingual response quality
- Over-refusal and under-refusal
- Grounded generation
- Source attribution
- Sensitive information leakage
- Prompt injection resilience
- AI agent permissions
- Safety regression testing

### 7. Governance and Risk Management

- Risk identification and prioritization
- Impact assessment
- Mitigation controls
- Release approval
- Auditability
- Monitoring and reassessment
- Incident response
- Documentation and accountability

### Folder guidelines

- `data/` — synthetic or appropriately licensed datasets.
- `notebooks/` — exploratory analysis and experiments.
- `src/` — reusable implementation code.
- `tests/` — automated checks.
- `evaluations/` — evaluation cases and metric definitions.
- `reports/` — results, limitations, and recommendations.
- `templates/` — model cards, risk registers, and review checklists.
- `README.md` — purpose, methodology, results, limitations, and reproduction steps.

Do not upload private personal information, confidential company data, real credentials, or sensitive model outputs.

## 🧪 Example: A Responsible AI Evaluation Workflow

Consider a hypothetical model that predicts whether a loan application should be approved.

1. **Define the use case:** Document what the model is intended to support and who may be affected.
2. **Inspect the dataset:** Review its source, consent, representation, missing values, and label quality.
3. **Establish a baseline:** Train a simple model and record standard performance metrics.
4. **Evaluate groups:** Compare relevant error rates and selection outcomes where lawful and appropriate.
5. **Investigate disparities:** Examine data quality, sample sizes, feature choices, and possible confounding factors.
6. **Explore explanations:** Inspect model behavior without treating feature explanations as causal proof.
7. **Document limitations:** Record known risks, uncertainty, and unsuitable uses.
8. **Recommend safeguards:** Consider human review, monitoring, appeal mechanisms, and additional validation.
9. **Re-evaluate:** Repeat the assessment after meaningful data or model changes.

Use synthetic data for learning. This experiment does not establish that a real lending system is legally compliant or suitable for consequential decisions.

## 📊 Example Evaluation Metrics

| Area | Example Metrics or Checks |
|---|---|
| Model utility | Accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score |
| Group performance | Group-wise false-positive and false-negative rates |
| Fairness | Demographic parity difference, equalized odds difference |
| Calibration | Brier score, calibration curves |
| Privacy | Data minimization checks, re-identification risk analysis |
| Explainability | Explanation stability and usefulness |
| RAG grounding | Citation support and evidence relevance |
| LLM safety | Category-specific failure rate and appropriate refusal rate |
| Reliability | Error rate, latency, successful recovery rate |
| Governance | Risk-control coverage, review completion, incident closure |

Metric selection depends on the application, data, affected groups, and consequences of errors. A favorable metric alone does not establish that a system is responsible.

## 💡 Guiding Principle

**Understand the use case → Identify potential harms → Evaluate with appropriate evidence → Protect privacy → Document limitations → Apply safeguards → Monitor and improve.**

Responsible AI is not a single tool or a checkbox. It is an ongoing engineering practice that combines technical evaluation, clear documentation, human judgment, and accountability.
